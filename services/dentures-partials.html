Dentures & partials

Missing several teeth — or all of them — doesn't mean settling for dentures that look like dentures. Modern full and partial dentures are lighter, more natural, and fit better than the ones your parents had.

Full and partial options

A partial denture replaces several missing teeth while keeping your healthy ones; a full denture restores an entire arch. Both are custom-made from impressions and shade-matching so the result suits your face — teeth sized and arranged for you, not from a catalog.

The fit is everything

A denture you don't wear is a denture that failed. We take fit seriously: careful impressions, adjustment visits included, and honest conversation about what dentures can and can't do before you commit.

Want them to never move? Ask about implants.

Implant-supported dentures snap onto two or more dental implants — no slipping, no adhesive, and dramatically better chewing. Dr. Khan is Implant Continuum trained and will tell you plainly whether you're a candidate and what it costs before any decision.

Not sure which option fits your situation? The exam is where you get real answers — request a visit and we'll walk through it together.

Common questions

What is the difference between a full and a partial denture?+

A full denture replaces every tooth in an arch. A partial replaces several missing teeth while keeping your healthy ones, clipping to them for support.

Will my dentures look like real teeth?+

They are made from impressions and shade-matched to you, with each tooth sized and arranged individually rather than picked from a catalog.

What is an implant-supported denture?+

A denture that snaps onto two or more dental implants. No slipping, no adhesive, and much better chewing. It requires a candidacy exam, since implants need adequate bone.

How long does it take to get dentures?+

Several visits: impressions, a try-in to check fit and appearance, delivery, then adjustment visits, which are included.

Will they feel uncomfortable at first?+

Some adjustment is normal in the first weeks. Adjustment visits are part of the process so sore spots get fixed rather than tolerated.

Can I upgrade my existing denture to implants?+

Often, yes. An exam with imaging shows whether there is enough bone to support implants, or whether grafting would be needed first.
