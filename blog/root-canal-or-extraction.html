Root canal or extraction: how the decision is made

5 min read · Fort Worth Dentist

Faced with a badly damaged tooth, patients often ask to "just pull it" — it sounds faster and cheaper. Sometimes it is the right call. Often it's the more expensive decision in disguise.

The default is saving the tooth

Nothing replaces a natural tooth as well as the natural tooth. Its root maintains your jawbone, its position keeps neighbors from drifting, and it costs nothing to keep. A root canal removes infected pulp from inside the tooth so the structure itself can stay — and with modern anesthetic and technique, it feels far closer to having a filling done than the reputation suggests.

When extraction genuinely is better

  • The tooth is cracked below the gumline. A vertical root fracture can't be sealed, and treating it only delays the inevitable at your expense.
  • Too little tooth remains. A crown needs structure to hold onto; without enough, the restoration fails.
  • Advanced gum disease. If the supporting bone is largely gone, saving the crown of the tooth doesn't help.
  • Poor long-term prognosis in a broader plan. Sometimes a tooth could be saved but shouldn't be, because it's compromising a larger restorative plan.

What extraction really costs

An extraction is cheaper than a root canal and crown on the day. But a gap doesn't stay a gap: neighboring teeth tip into the space, the opposing tooth over-erupts, bite forces redistribute, and the jawbone in that area begins to shrink. Replacing the tooth later with an implant or bridge typically costs more than saving it would have — and if the bone has receded, grafting gets added to the bill.

How we decide with you

Imaging shows the extent of decay, the shape of the roots, and the bone level around them. We test whether the nerve is alive. You see the same pictures we do, and you get both plans with real numbers — save it, or remove and replace it — because this is a decision about your money and your mouth, not a recommendation to accept on faith.

Common questions

Is it better to have a root canal or pull the tooth?+

Saving the natural tooth is usually better when it's structurally sound, because extraction leads to bone loss and drifting teeth, and replacement typically costs more than the root canal would have. Extraction is the right call when the tooth is cracked below the gumline, too little structure remains, or gum disease has destroyed the supporting bone.

Are root canals painful?+

With modern anesthetic and technique, most patients report it feeling much like having a filling done. The pain people associate with root canals is usually the infection that made the treatment necessary.

What happens if I don't replace an extracted tooth?+

Neighboring teeth tend to drift into the space, the opposing tooth can over-erupt, and the jawbone in that area gradually shrinks — which can complicate and add cost to replacing the tooth later.
