What to do in a dental emergency

5 min read · Fort Worth Dentist

The first thirty minutes matter more than most people realize — particularly for a knocked-out tooth. Here's what to do while you're calling us, and how to tell what needs attention today versus what can safely wait.

Call first, always

Before anything else: 817-926-1300. The team can triage over the phone, tell you exactly what to do in the moment, and hold same-day time during office hours for genuine urgencies. A web form can't do any of that at the speed a dental emergency needs.

Knocked-out permanent tooth

This is the one true race against the clock. Pick the tooth up by the crown, never the root. If it's dirty, rinse it briefly with milk or saline — don't scrub it, and don't use soap. If you can, place it gently back in the socket and hold it there. If you can't, keep it in milk (or the patient's own saliva; water is a poor last resort) and get to us immediately. Teeth replanted within the first hour have dramatically better odds.

Severe toothache

Rinse with warm salt water and floss gently around the tooth in case something is lodged. Over-the-counter pain relief helps; never place aspirin directly against the gum — it burns the tissue. A cold compress on the outside of the cheek reduces swelling. Pain that keeps you awake or throbs on its own usually means the nerve is involved, and that needs treatment, not waiting.

Facial swelling — read this part carefully

Swelling means infection, and infection in the mouth can move fast. Call us the same day. But if swelling is affecting your breathing or swallowing, or you have a fever with it, go to the emergency room or call 911. That's beyond what a dental office can safely manage, and it's genuinely dangerous.

Broken or chipped tooth

Save any pieces, rinse gently with warm water, and use a cold compress for swelling. If a sharp edge is cutting your tongue, dental wax or a piece of sugarless gum over it helps until you're seen. Read more about broken and chipped tooth repair.

Lost filling or crown

Usually urgent-ish rather than urgent. Keep the crown if you have it — it can often be recemented. Avoid chewing on that side, and don't use household glue, which contaminates the tooth and can turn a simple recementation into a much bigger repair.

Not sure whether it's an emergency? Call and describe it. We'd rather tell you it can wait until Tuesday than have you sit at home with something that can't.

Common questions

What counts as a dental emergency?+

Severe or worsening tooth pain, facial swelling, a knocked-out or broken tooth, bleeding that won't stop, or a suspected abscess. If swelling affects breathing or swallowing, go to the emergency room or call 911 instead.

What should I do with a knocked-out tooth?+

Handle it by the crown, not the root. Rinse briefly with milk or saline without scrubbing, and either place it back in the socket or keep it in milk. Get to a dentist immediately — the first hour matters enormously.

Can I get a same-day dental appointment for pain?+

Call 817-926-1300 — same-day appointments are held during office hours for genuine urgencies, and the team can triage over the phone.
