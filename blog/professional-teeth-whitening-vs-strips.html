Professional whitening vs. drugstore strips

4 min read · Fort Worth Dentist

Strips are cheap and they do something. Professional whitening is faster, more even, and better controlled. Here's the actual difference, and the honest limits of both.

Concentration and contact

Professional whitening uses higher-concentration gel than anything sold over the counter, and — this is the part people underestimate — custom-fitted trays that hold the gel against your teeth and off your gums. A one-size strip doesn't follow your arch; it misses the curved surfaces between teeth and leaks onto tissue, which is where most sensitivity and blotchiness come from.

What whitening can't do

This is where honesty saves you money. Whitening lightens natural enamel only. It does not change the color of crowns, veneers, or tooth-colored fillings — so if you whiten teeth beside an existing crown, the crown will suddenly look darker and may need replacing to match. Some stains, particularly from tetracycline, respond poorly to any bleaching. And discoloration from a dying nerve needs treating, not whitening.

Sensitivity, managed properly

Some sensitivity during whitening is normal and temporary. It's controlled with lower-concentration gel, shorter wear times, and desensitizing products — adjustments a dentist can make and a box of strips can't. If your teeth are already sensitive, say so before starting rather than pushing through.

Start with a cleaning

Whitening works on clean enamel. A professional cleaning first removes surface stain and plaque so the gel contacts the tooth evenly — patients are sometimes surprised by how much of what they wanted removed comes off at the cleaning itself.

If the shape or proportion of your teeth bothers you as much as the shade, that's a smile-design conversation rather than a whitening one — see The Veneer Suite.

Common questions

Is professional teeth whitening better than strips?+

Professional whitening uses higher-concentration gel with custom-fitted trays that keep it on the teeth and off the gums, producing faster and more even results with better-managed sensitivity.

Will whitening work on crowns and fillings?+

No. Whitening lightens natural enamel only. Existing crowns, veneers, and tooth-colored fillings keep their original shade, which is worth planning around before you start.

Does teeth whitening damage enamel?+

Professionally supervised whitening at appropriate concentrations doesn't damage enamel. Temporary sensitivity is common and manageable by adjusting concentration and wear time.
