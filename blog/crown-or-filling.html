Do I need a crown or will a filling do?

4 min read · Fort Worth Dentist

It's a fair question to ask, and a fair one to want justified — a crown costs considerably more than a filling. Here's the actual engineering behind the decision.

The structural difference

A filling replaces missing tooth material and relies on the remaining healthy tooth around it for strength. A crown encircles the tooth and takes over that structural job itself. So the question isn't really "how big is the cavity" — it's "how much healthy tooth is left to do the work."

When a filling is the right answer

Small to moderate decay with solid walls of enamel remaining, particularly on surfaces that don't take the heaviest bite forces. Modern composite bonds well and, in the right situation, lasts years. Choosing a crown here would mean removing healthy tooth structure for no gain — which is its own kind of harm.

When a crown is genuinely necessary

  • After root canal treatment on a back tooth — the tooth becomes more brittle and reliably fractures without full coverage.
  • A cracked tooth — a filling can wedge a crack wider; a crown holds it together.
  • A large old filling failing — when more filling than tooth remains, replacing like with like just resets the clock on the same problem.
  • A significant break, where there isn't enough structure for bonding to hold.

What happens if you choose wrong

Filling a tooth that needed a crown often ends the same way: the tooth fractures, sometimes below the gumline, and what would have been a crown becomes an extraction and an implant. That's the scenario worth avoiding, and it's why we'd rather show you the photo and explain the reasoning than simply hand down a recommendation.

Ask to see the intraoral photo of your tooth. Any dentist should be glad to show it and walk you through why a crown is or isn't warranted. More on dental crowns.

Common questions

How do I know if I need a crown or a filling?+

It depends on how much healthy tooth structure remains, not just the size of the cavity. Fillings rely on the surrounding tooth for strength; crowns take over that structural role. Root-canal-treated back teeth, cracked teeth, and teeth with large failing fillings generally need crowns.

How many visits does a crown take?+

Most crowns take two visits: one for preparation and a digital scan, and a second to fit and bond the finished crown. A temporary crown is worn in between.

Can a filling replace a crown to save money?+

Sometimes, but if the tooth genuinely needs full coverage, a filling risks the tooth fracturing later — occasionally below the gumline, which can mean losing the tooth entirely.
